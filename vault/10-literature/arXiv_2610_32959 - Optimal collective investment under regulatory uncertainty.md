# Optimal collective investment under regulatory uncertainty

## Abstract

> We study optimal collective investment subject to a Value-at-Risk (VaR)-type constraint that
> accounts for uncertainty about future regulatory requirements. The constraint incorporates
> several possible wealth thresholds and imposes a minimum weighted average probability of meeting
> them. We characterize the optimal terminal wealth, which combines regions of constant wealth at
> regulatory thresholds with Merton-type investment regions. Unlike a classical VaR constraint
> with only one threshold, our formulation generates protection against several thresholds. For
> two possible thresholds, we identify when both are protected and when protection concentrates on
> the larger one. Numerical results show that the regulatory constraint can substantially reshape
> the optimal payoff and generate large welfare losses relative to unconstrained investment when
> the required compliance probability is high, even the smaller wealth threshold is high, or
> initial capital is limited. In heterogeneous investor pools, welfare losses depend strongly on
> the composition of the risk aversions and the Pareto weights governing risk sharing. Losses are
> particularly pronounced when highly risk-averse investors dominate the pool. Moreover, investors
> with smaller capital contributions can bear disproportionately large shares of the total welfare
> loss, while larger contributors may bear less than their capital-proportional share. Thus,
> regulatory uncertainty not only reduces aggregate welfare but can also create substantial
> disparities in how its costs are distributed among investors.
