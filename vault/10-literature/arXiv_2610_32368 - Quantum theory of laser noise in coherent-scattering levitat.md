# Quantum theory of laser noise in coherent-scattering levitated optomechanics

## Abstract

> State purity is a key resource for experimental tests of quantum mechanics with massive objects.
> Cavity cooling of a levitated nanoparticle via coherent scattering stands out as a strategy to
> prepare highly pure states of its roto-translational motion. In several cases, though, the
> performance of cooling by coherent scattering is limited by the classical fluctuations of the
> laser field. Here we present a quantum theory of coherent-scattering experiments which includes
> the effect of the laser amplitude and phase noise. We derive a master equation for the noise-
> averaged particle's dynamics, valid for both center-of-mass and rotational degrees of freedom,
> and quantify the cooling performance for different levels of noise in state-of-the-art
> experiments. We compute approximate analytical expressions for the laser-noise-induced heating
> rates and excess phonon number in both regimes of weak and strong optomechanical coupling.
> Moreover, our model can be applied to laser noise processes with generic power spectral
> densities and finite correlation times. This work offers a toolbox to quantify and optimize the
> state purity in coherent-scattering experiments, and opens new avenues towards the preparation
> of quantum states with levitated systems.
