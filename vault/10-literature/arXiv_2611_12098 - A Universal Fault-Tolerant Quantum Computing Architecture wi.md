# A Universal Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing Architecture with Globally Driven Atom Arrays

## Abstract

> Scalable quantum computation requires reconciling programmability with simple, high-fidelity
> control. Here, we propose a universal fault-tolerant architecture using globally driven dual-
> species atom arrays. In a hybrid neutral-atom system, interspecies Rydberg interactions enable
> qubit addressability without locally addressed quantum gates. We construct a universal native
> instruction set, Global Rydberg Atom Instruction Language (GRAIL), and show that crosstalk
> errors can be suppressed to the $10^{-4}$ level. We compile surface-code error correction and
> magic state cultivation into GRAIL, and parallelize the compilation to suppress crosstalk. Under
> a hardware-inferred circuit-level noise model, we obtain an error-correction threshold of
> $1.43\%$ for the resulting quantum memory and a logical error rate below $10^{-9}$ for magic
> states cultivated at an experimentally accessible Rydberg Rabi frequency. By replacing local
> gate addressing with atom-mediated selectivity, this architecture removes the associated
> calibration requirements, offering a route to simpler control for large-scale fault-tolerant
> neutral-atom computation.
